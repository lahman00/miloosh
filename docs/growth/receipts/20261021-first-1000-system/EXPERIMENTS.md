# Three acquisition experiments, ready for approval (nothing started)

Common rules: no paid media, no automated engagement, no unsolicited messages, each post published once through the established approval and identity checks, every link carries `utm_source`, `utm_medium=organic_social`, a per-experiment `utm_campaign` and a per-post `utm_content`. Success is judged on **strict-qualified** sessions and **site hand-offs from qualified sessions**, read with `npm run growth:daily-report` after 7 complete days. A post is not evidence of traffic. Thresholds are never changed to make a result look better.

Baseline reality (live reads, 2026-10-20): LinkedIn Page has 21 followers and its last five posts earned 4, 1, 2, 9 and 4 organic impressions. In the last 7 days the site recorded 0 strict-qualified sessions and 31 probable. Organic LinkedIn alone cannot be expected to supply a measurable audience, so each experiment pairs the Page post with one or two **relevant public discussions** where a source-backed answer is genuinely useful. Those need an authenticated session and the owner's approval per thread.

## A. Outbound sales tool selection (Apollo.io, with Salesloft, Outreach, Cognism, Sales Navigator)
- **Audience:** a founder or first sales hire at a small B2B company choosing a prospecting tool.
- **Problem:** the price models differ (seat vs credits vs "talk to sales") and a buyer cannot compare them on a list of features.
- **Landing page:** `/compare/apollo-io-vs-linkedin-sales-navigator` (editable, live, verified pricing 2026-10-19; Apollo.io payout verified). Alternate: `/compare/apollo-io-vs-salesloft`.
- **Channel:** one LinkedIn Page post plus up to two public discussion answers on prospecting-tool selection (owner-approved, no link unless the thread asks for sources).
- **Hypothesis:** a post that states the verified price models and limits attracts at least a few buyers who open the pricing table and the vendor CTA, which a generic feature post did not.
- **Success metric (21 days):** at least 5 strict-qualified sessions landing on the Apollo comparisons with at least 1 affiliate hand-off from a qualified session. This is deliberately modest; it is a proof of path, not a revenue target.
- **Stop or revise:** after 14 days with fewer than 2 strict-qualified sessions from all channels combined on these pages, stop posting and revise the channel (discussions or a different page); do not change the page during the window.
- **Tracking:** `utm_campaign=exp_a_sales_stack_oct2026`, first-party funnel by landing page and channel, site hand-offs by partner. Partner-side clicks and any lead or sale come only from a PartnerStack export the owner supplies.
- **Cannot be measured today:** search volume and ranking for these pages, partner-network clicks, leads, sales and commissions, and whether any visitor is a real buyer rather than a curious reader.

## B. Customer support phone line (KrispCall vs Dialpad / Nextiva)
- **Audience:** an owner or operations lead at a 1 to 20 person team that needs a business phone for support or sales calls.
- **Problem:** vendors list annual-term prices as headline figures; monthly billing costs more and some plans need a 12-month term.
- **Landing page:** `/compare/krispcall-vs-dialpad` (editable, live; both billing bases now stated). Alternate: `/compare/krispcall-vs-nextiva`.
- **Channel:** one LinkedIn Page post plus up to two public discussions about small-team phone systems.
- **Hypothesis:** a post that states annual vs monthly pricing plainly earns clicks from buyers who have been misled by headline prices elsewhere.
- **Success metric (21 days):** at least 5 strict-qualified sessions on the two KrispCall pages with at least 1 KrispCall hand-off from a qualified session.
- **Stop or revise:** same 14-day rule as A.
- **Tracking:** `utm_campaign=exp_b_support_phone_oct2026`; same funnel report; KrispCall network report supplied by the owner.
- **Cannot be measured today:** demand volume, KrispCall network clicks and conversions, whether the visitor is a support team or an individual.
- **Scope note:** SupportBee, Intercom, Freshdesk, Help Scout and Front have the historical search demand in support software, but none is an active partner (SupportBee is only an unverified invitation), so a helpdesk-tool experiment would produce no commission.

## C. App-builder evaluation (Lovable, Playcode, Wix)
- **Audience:** a founder or non-developer choosing an AI app builder after a first demo.
- **Problem:** export, custom domain and usage-based costs surface after the demo.
- **Landing page:** `/software/lovable` and `/software/playcode` are in an observation window until 2026-11-12, and neither Lovable nor Playcode is an active partner. **This experiment cannot produce a Miloosh commission** unless Wix (Impact, payout not verified) is the pivot.
- **Status:** **not ready to start.** Starting it would add traffic to pages whose measurement window is still running and would test a topic that has already been posted three times (Facebook 2026-10-19, X 2026-10-15, LinkedIn earlier). Recommended: wait for 2026-11-12, and decide with the owner whether a Wix-based version is worth running once the Impact payout rail is verified.
- **Success metric / stop rule when it starts:** 5 strict-qualified sessions in 14 days from a non-repeated post; stop after 2 posts without a strict-qualified session.
- **Tracking:** `utm_campaign=exp_c_app_builder_nov2026`.
- **Cannot be measured today:** any partner outcome for Lovable or Playcode (no program), Wix network clicks and sales, and the effect of earlier posts (organic reach is in single digits).
